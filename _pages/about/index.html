I am a 4th-year PhD student in Linguistics at New York University (NYU), where I work with Prof. Liina Pylkkänen at the Neuroscience of Language Lab (NeLLab).

My research investigates the interface between language structure and meaning in the brain. I use behavioral and neurophysiological methods, including EEG and MEG, to investigate the processes involved in linguistic representation and computation as they unfold in real time. My current work examines structure building at multiple levels of linguistic representation, from morphological structure within words to syntactic and semantic composition across words.

Before coming to NYU, I earned my master’s degree in Linguistics at the Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ), where I worked with Prof. Aniela Improta França at the Syntactic Access Lab. I found that diachronic lexical relations between derived words do not impact their synchronic lexical access. I came to this conclusion by collecting and analyzing EEG and behavioral data.
