I am a 4th-year PhD student in Linguistics at New York University (NYU), where I work with [Prof. Liina Pylkkänen](https://wp.nyu.edu/neurolinglab/people/liina-pylkkanen/) at the [Neuroscience of Language Lab (NeLLab)](https://wp.nyu.edu/nellab/).

My research investigates the interface between language structure and meaning in the brain. I use behavioral and neurophysiological methods, including EEG and MEG, to investigate the processes involved in linguistic representation and computation as they unfold in real time, on the millisecond timescale. My current work examines structure building at multiple levels of linguistic representation, from morphological structure and interpretation within words to syntactic and semantic composition across words.

Before coming to NYU, I earned my master's degree in Linguistics at the Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ), where I worked with [Prof. Aniela Improta França](http://lattes.cnpq.br/3217127142809144) at the [Syntactic Access Lab](https://acesin.com.br/). There, I studied the lexical access of words whose morphological history is synchronically opaque. For example, Portuguese *restaurante* ("restaurant") is derived from *restaurar* ("to restore"), but speakers do not ordinarily recognize the two as related. Using EEG and behavioral measures, my results supported the conclusion that these diachronic relations do not influence synchronic lexical access, consistent with these words being represented as independent lexical entries in the mental lexicon.
